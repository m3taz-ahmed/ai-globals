# Slop patterns

Named patterns to detect and fix in generated content. Each pattern includes the name, example, and fix.

## 1. Binary contrasts
**Example:** "This is not X. It's Y." / "The question isn't X, it's Y."
**Fix:** State Y directly. "The eval matters more than the model."

## 2. Throat-clearing openers
**Example:** "Here's the thing," "Let me be clear," "I'll be honest."
**Fix:** Cut and state the point.

## 3. Faux-insight setups
**Example:** "This is the part most people skip," "What most people get wrong."
**Fix:** Cut the setup, make the claim stand alone.

## 4. Colon reveals
**Example:** "The detail that makes it work: a separate agent grades it."
**Fix:** Rewrite as a plain sentence. "A separate agent does the grading, which is what makes it work."

## 5. Superficial analysis
**Example:** "highlighting," "underscoring," "reflecting," "showcasing."
**Fix:** Replace with concrete consequence. "so users can find old drafts without leaving the editor."

## 6. Importance puffery
**Example:** "Stands as a testament," "marks a pivotal moment," "plays a vital role."
**Fix:** State the fact. "The launch is the company's first paid product."

## 7. Interpretive metadiscourse
**Example:** "That last part matters more than it sounds," "The key point is," "As you can see."
**Fix:** Delete the aside or replace with support already in the content.

## 8. Weasel attribution
**Example:** "Experts agree," "industry reports suggest," "many argue," "widely regarded as."
**Fix:** Name the source or cut the claim.

## 9. Fake-strong verbs
**Example:** "The app serves as a centralized hub for sponsor management."
**Fix:** "The app tracks sponsors, drafts, due dates, and approvals in one place."

## 10. Synonym cycling
**Example:** "The agent reviews the draft. The assistant scores the piece. The tool suggests fixes."
**Fix:** "The agent reviews the draft, scores it, and suggests fixes."

## 11. Negative listing
**Example:** "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z."
**Fix:** Just say Z.

## 12. Dramatic fragmentation
**Example:** "X. And Y. And Z." or "That's it. That's the whole thing."
**Fix:** Use complete sentences.

## 13. Robotic rhythm
**Example:** Repeated sentence shapes, identical paragraph structures, stacked punchy fragments.
**Fix:** Vary the shape only when it helps the point.

## 14. Rhetorical setups
**Example:** "What if I told you...", "Think about it:", "Plot twist:", self-answered "Question? Answer." pairs.
**Fix:** Drop and make the point.

## 15. Fake-profound kickers
**Example:** Final "deep" line that turns the point into a metaphor or aphorism.
**Fix:** Delete it. End on the clearest concrete sentence already in the draft.

## 16. Summary-recap endings
**Example:** "In conclusion," "Ultimately," "Overall," or a final paragraph restating the piece.
**Fix:** End on the last concrete point, takeaway, or next action.

## 17. Formatting slop
**Example:** Emoji in headings, bold sprinkled mid-sentence, bullet lists where prose reads better, headers over two-sentence sections.
**Fix:** Format follows content, not decorates it.

## 18. Em dashes
**Example:** Using em dashes as a default rhythm crutch, clusters of dashes.
**Fix:** 0 in short copy. 1-2 max in longer drafts only when they clearly beat commas, periods, or parentheses.
